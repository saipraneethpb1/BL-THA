    What is a sitemap?

    A sitemap is a list of pages published by a website, usually at /sitemap.xml. Paste the full link, including https://. Nested sitemaps are checked too. You can review the results before adding a source and stop an active crawl at any time.

    Knowledge status

    Each source is re-checked on its own schedule (set on the source, in the Knowledge panel); only changed pages are fetched again. "Run refresh now" checks every source.

    Self-maintained prompts

    The agent writes and maintains its own query-rewrite prompt (the step that turns customer wording into help-center terms). Every version is tested on 168 wordings of 42 questions: as asked, with synonyms ("custom", "customize", "customise"), misspelled, and with other names for the same feature. A question passes when every wording finds the same right article. A new version goes live only if it does better on questions it never saw.

    Tests the live prompt, writes a new one from what failed, tests that: a few cents, about 10 minutes.

    How automatic checks work

    Pages that return 404 and are missing from their sitemap are archived immediately. Valid redirects are followed and re-indexed at the new address. Archived content is kept and can be restored.

    Archived KB

    Pages taken out of normal answers: gone from their site, or archived after review. They keep their text and passages and can be searched here on their own. Restore brings a page back: it's fetched again at the next check.

    Duplicates and change history

    A page found twice is stored and searched once: the same address spelled differently, the same page in two sitemaps, or the same text at two URLs. Every change to a page is recorded.
